## What is Matter?

- Anything that occupies space and has mass is called **matter**
- Matter has 5 basic properties: mass, volume, shape, density, temperature

## States of Matter

### Solid
- Definite shape and volume
- Particles tightly packed, vibrate in fixed positions
- Cannot be compressed
- Examples: Ice, wood, iron, diamond

### Liquid
- Definite volume, no definite shape (takes shape of container)
- Particles close together but can move past each other
- Slightly compressible
- Examples: Water, milk, oil, mercury

### Gas
- No definite shape or volume
- Particles far apart, move randomly at high speed
- Highly compressible
- Examples: Air, oxygen, nitrogen, steam

### Plasma (4th state)
- Ionized gas at very high temperature
- Free electrons and ions
- Found in stars, lightning, neon signs

### Bose-Einstein Condensate (5th state)
- Formed at temperatures near absolute zero (0 K)
- Particles occupy the same quantum state

## Changes of State

### Melting (Fusion)
- Solid → Liquid
- Absorbs heat energy
- Melting point: temperature at which solid melts
- Example: Ice melts at 0°C

### Boiling (Vaporization)
- Liquid → Gas
- Absorbs heat energy
- Boiling point: temperature at which liquid boils
- Example: Water boils at 100°C at 1 atm

### Condensation
- Gas → Liquid
- Releases heat energy
- Example: Steam condenses on cold surface

### Freezing (Solidification)
- Liquid → Solid
- Releases heat energy
- Freezing point: temperature at which liquid freezes
- Example: Water freezes at 0°C

### Sublimation
- Solid → Gas (without becoming liquid)
- Example: Camphor, naphthalene, dry ice (solid CO₂)

## Evaporation

- Surface phenomenon - occurs only at the surface of liquid
- Occurs at all temperatures (not just at boiling point)
- Rate of evaporation increases with:
  - Increase in temperature
  - Increase in surface area
  - Decrease in humidity
  - Increase in wind speed
- **Cooling effect**: Evaporation causes cooling because high-energy particles leave the surface

## Temperature

- **Temperature**: Measure of average kinetic energy of particles
- Measured by **thermometer**
- Scales: Celsius (°C), Fahrenheit (°F), Kelvin (K)
- SI unit: **Kelvin (K)**
- Absolute zero: 0 K = -273.15°C (lowest possible temperature)
- Normal body temperature: 37°C = 98.6°F = 310 K
- Normal boiling point of water: 100°C = 212°F = 373 K

## Heat

- **Heat**: Form of energy transferred from hotter to colder body
- SI unit: **Joule (J)**
- Calorific value: Heat released on complete combustion of 1 kg of fuel
  - LPG: 55000 kJ/kg
  - Kerosene: 45000 kJ/kg
  - Diesel: 45000 kJ/kg
  - Coal: 25000-33000 kJ/kg
  - Cow dung cake: 6000-8000 kJ/kg
  - Wood: 17000-22000 kJ/kg

## Humidity

- Amount of water vapor present in air
- High humidity → feel sticky and uncomfortable
- 100% humidity → air is saturated → no more evaporation → rain
