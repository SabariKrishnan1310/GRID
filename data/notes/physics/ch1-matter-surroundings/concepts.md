# Matter in Our Surroundings — Detailed Concepts

## 1.1 Matter — Definition and Properties

- **Matter**: Anything that occupies space and has mass is called matter
  - Matter exists in various forms: solid, liquid, gas, plasma, Bose-Einstein condensate
  - All matter is made up of tiny particles called atoms, molecules, or ions
  - These particles have spaces between them, are in constant motion, and attract each other

- **Five basic properties of matter**:
  1. Mass: Amount of matter in an object
  2. Volume: Space occupied by matter
  3. Shape: External form of matter
  4. Density: Mass per unit volume
  5. Temperature: Measure of average kinetic energy of particles

## 1.2 Solid State

- **Solid**: Matter that has definite shape and definite volume
  - Particles are packed very closely in a regular, ordered arrangement
  - Particles vibrate in fixed positions but do not move from place to place
  - Very strong intermolecular forces of attraction between particles
  - Cannot be compressed (incompressible)
  - Have high density and high melting/boiling points
  - Examples: Ice, diamond, iron, copper, wood, salt, rubber

- **Characteristics of solids**:
  - Definite shape: Solid retains its shape irrespective of the container
  - Definite volume: Solid occupies a fixed amount of space
  - Incompressible: Cannot be compressed because particles are very close
  - Rigid: Has a fixed structure
  - High density: Particles are packed closely together

## 1.3 Liquid State

- **Liquid**: Matter that has definite volume but no definite shape
  - Particles are close together but not in fixed positions
  - Particles can move past each other (flow)
  - Intermolecular forces are weaker than in solids but stronger than in gases
  - Slightly compressible
  - Lower density than solids (except water, which is denser than ice)
  - Examples: Water, milk, kerosene, mercury, petrol, oil

- **Characteristics of liquids**:
  - No definite shape: Takes the shape of the container
  - Definite volume: Occupies a fixed amount of space
  - Fluid: Can flow and change shape
  - Slightly compressible: Particles have small spaces between them
  - Lower boiling point than corresponding solids

## 1.4 Gaseous State

- **Gas**: Matter that has no definite shape and no definite volume
  - Particles are far apart with no regular arrangement
  - Particles move randomly at very high speeds
  - Very weak intermolecular forces of attraction
  - Highly compressible
  - Low density and low boiling point
  - Fills the entire container uniformly
  - Examples: Air, oxygen, nitrogen, hydrogen, carbon dioxide, steam

- **Characteristics of gases**:
  - No definite shape: Fills the shape of the container
  - No definite volume: Expands to fill entire container
  - Highly compressible: Large spaces between particles
  - Exert pressure uniformly in all directions
  - Low density: Particles are far apart

## 1.5 Plasma State

- **Plasma**: Fourth state of matter
  - Formed when gas is heated to very high temperatures or subjected to strong electromagnetic fields
  - Electrons are stripped from atoms, creating a mixture of free electrons and positively charged ions
  - Conducts electricity due to presence of free charged particles
  - Found in stars (including our Sun), lightning, neon signs, fluorescent tubes, TV screens
  - Also called ionized gas
  - Most common state of matter in the universe (stars are mostly plasma)

## 1.6 Bose-Einstein Condensate

- **BEC**: Fifth state of matter
  - Predicted by Satyendra Nath Bose and Albert Einstein in 1924
  - First created in 1995 by Eric Cornell and Carl Wieman
  - Formed when matter is cooled to temperatures very close to absolute zero (0 K or -273.15°C)
  - At this temperature, particles lose their individual identity and occupy the same quantum state
  - All particles behave as a single entity (superatom)
  - Used in quantum computing and precision measurements

## 1.7 Interconversion of States of Matter

- **Melting (Fusion)**: Change of state from solid to liquid
  - Occurs at the melting point of the substance
  - Energy is absorbed (endothermic process)
  - Example: Ice melts at 0°C to form water

- **Boiling (Vaporization)**: Change of state from liquid to gas
  - Occurs at the boiling point of the substance
  - Energy is absorbed (endothermic process)
  - Example: Water boils at 100°C at 1 atm pressure to form steam

- **Condensation**: Change of state from gas to liquid
  - Energy is released (exothermic process)
  - Example: Steam condenses on a cold surface to form water droplets

- **Freezing (Solidification)**: Change of state from liquid to solid
  - Occurs at the freezing point of the substance
  - Energy is released (exothermic process)
  - Example: Water freezes at 0°C to form ice

- **Sublimation**: Change of state from solid directly to gas
  - Energy is absorbed (endothermic process)
  - Example: Camphor, naphthalene, dry ice (solid CO₂) sublime directly

- **Deposition**: Change of state from gas directly to solid
  - Energy is released (exothermic process)
  - Example: Frost formation in winter

## 1.8 Evaporation

- **Evaporation**: Surface phenomenon where liquid converts to gas at temperatures below the boiling point
  - Occurs at all temperatures, not just at the boiling point
  - Only surface particles escape (unlike boiling where entire liquid converts)
  - Rate of evaporation depends on: temperature, surface area, humidity, wind speed

- **Factors affecting evaporation**:
  1. Increase in temperature → faster evaporation
  2. Increase in surface area → faster evaporation
  3. Decrease in humidity → faster evaporation
  4. Increase in wind speed → faster evaporation

- **Cooling effect of evaporation**:
  - High-energy particles escape from the surface of the liquid
  - Remaining particles have lower average kinetic energy
  - Temperature of the remaining liquid decreases
  - Example: Sweating cools the body, earthen pot keeps water cool

## 1.9 Temperature and Heat

- **Temperature**: Measure of average kinetic energy of particles
  - SI unit: Kelvin (K)
  - Celsius scale: °C = K - 273.15
  - Fahrenheit scale: °F = (9/5 × °C) + 32
  - Absolute zero: 0 K = -273.15°C (lowest possible temperature)

- **Heat**: Energy transferred from a body at higher temperature to a body at lower temperature
  - SI unit: Joule (J)
  - Heat always flows from hotter body to colder body
  - Amount of heat depends on: temperature difference, mass, specific heat capacity

- **Calorific value**: Amount of heat energy released on complete combustion of 1 kg of fuel
  - LPG: 55,000 kJ/kg
  - Kerosene: 45,000 kJ/kg
  - Diesel: 45,000 kJ/kg
  - Coal: 25,000-33,000 kJ/kg
  - Cow dung cake: 6,000-8,000 kJ/kg
  - Wood: 17,000-22,000 kJ/kg

## 1.10 Humidity

- **Humidity**: Amount of water vapor present in the air
  - High humidity → uncomfortable, sticky feeling
  - Low humidity → dry feeling, fast evaporation
  - 100% humidity → air is saturated → no more evaporation possible → rain
  - Measured by hygrometer
