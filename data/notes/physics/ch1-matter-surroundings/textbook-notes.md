## 1.1 Matter

- Anything that occupies space and has mass
- Exists in 5 states: solid, liquid, gas, plasma, Bose-Einstein condensate
- Made of tiny particles (atoms, molecules, ions)
- Particles have spaces between them
- Particles are in constant random motion
- Particles attract each other

## 1.2 Solid

- Definite shape and definite volume
- Particles tightly packed in regular arrangement
- Particles vibrate in fixed positions
- Very strong forces of attraction between particles
- Cannot be compressed
- Have high density and high boiling point
- Examples: Ice, diamond, iron, copper, wood, salt

## 1.3 Liquid

- Definite volume but no definite shape (takes shape of container)
- Particles are close together but not in fixed positions
- Can flow (fluid)
- Particles move past each other
- Forces of attraction are weaker than solids
- Slightly compressible
- Lower density than solids
- Examples: Water, milk, kerosene, mercury, petrol

## 1.4 Gas

- No definite shape or volume (fills entire container)
- Particles are far apart with no regular arrangement
- Move randomly at very high speeds
- Very weak forces of attraction between particles
- Highly compressible
- Low density and low boiling point
- Examples: Air, oxygen, nitrogen, hydrogen, carbon dioxide, steam

## 1.5 Plasma

- Fourth state of matter
- Superheated gas where electrons are separated from atoms
- Contains free electrons and positively charged ions
- Found in stars (Sun), lightning, neon signs, fluorescent tubes
- Conducts electricity
- Also called ionized gas

## 1.6 Bose-Einstein Condensate

- Fifth state of matter
- Formed when matter is cooled to temperatures very close to absolute zero (0 K)
- At this temperature, particles occupy the same quantum state
- Particles lose their individual identity
- Predicted by Einstein in 1924
- First created in 1995

## 1.7 Interconversion of States of Matter

- Matter can change from one state to another by changing temperature or pressure
- **Melting (Fusion)**: Solid → Liquid at melting point (absorbs heat)
- **Boiling (Vaporization)**: Liquid → Gas at boiling point (absorbs heat)
- **Condensation**: Gas → Liquid (releases heat)
- **Freezing**: Liquid → Solid at freezing point (releases heat)
- **Sublimation**: Solid → Gas directly (absorbs heat)
- **Deposition**: Gas → Solid directly (releases heat)

## 1.8 Evaporation

- Surface phenomenon — liquid converts to gas at temperatures below boiling point
- Occurs at all temperatures, not just at boiling point
- Rate depends on: temperature, surface area, humidity, wind speed
- **Cooling effect**: High-energy particles escape from surface, remaining liquid cools down
- Uses of evaporation: drying clothes, cooling in earthen pots, sweat evaporation

## 1.9 Temperature and Heat

- **Temperature**: Measure of average kinetic energy of particles
  - SI unit: Kelvin (K)
  - Celsius: °C = K - 273.15
  - Fahrenheit: °F = (9/5 × °C) + 32
- **Absolute zero**: 0 K = -273.15°C (lowest possible temperature)
- **Heat**: Energy transferred from hotter to colder body
  - SI unit: Joule (J)
  - Calorific value: Heat released per kg of fuel on complete combustion

## 1.11 Humidity

- Amount of water vapor present in air
- High humidity → uncomfortable, sticky feeling
- 100% humidity → air saturated → rain
- Low humidity → fast evaporation → dry feeling

## Activities

**Activity 1.1**: Place an iron nail, a glass rod, a chalk piece, rubber, aluminium strip in different containers — all occupy space (all are matter)
**Activity 1.2**: Pour water into different containers — takes shape of container but volume remains same
**Activity 1.3**: Release aerosol can near a candle — gas pushes flame away (particles in gas move freely)
**Activity 1.4**: Dissolve salt in water — volume of solution is less than sum of volumes of water and salt (particles have spaces between them)
**Activity 1.5**: Observe steam rising from boiling water — gas particles have very high kinetic energy
