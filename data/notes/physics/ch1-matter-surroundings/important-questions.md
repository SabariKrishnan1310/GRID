## Important Formulas

$$
\text{Temperature conversion}: K = °C + 273.15
$$

$$
°F = \frac{9}{5} \times °C + 32
$$

$$
°C = \frac{5}{9} \times (°F - 32)
$$

$$
\text{Density} = \frac{\text{Mass}}{\text{Volume}}
$$

$$
\text{Calorific value} = \frac{\text{Heat released}}{\text{Mass of fuel}}
$$

## Important Questions

### Q1. Define matter. Give its five states.

**Answer:**
- **Matter**: Anything that occupies space and has mass
- Five states: **Solid, Liquid, Gas, Plasma, Bose-Einstein Condensate**
- Solid: Definite shape and volume (ice, iron)
- Liquid: Definite volume, no definite shape (water, milk)
- Gas: No definite shape or volume (air, oxygen)
- Plasma: Ionized gas at high temperature (stars, lightning)
- BEC: Matter at near absolute zero temperature

### Q2. What is the difference between evaporation and boiling?

| Evaporation | Boiling |
|-------------|---------|
| Surface phenomenon | Bulk phenomenon |
| Occurs at all temperatures | Occurs at specific boiling point |
| Slow process | Fast process |
| Absorbs heat from surroundings | Absorbs heat from source |
| Example: Clothes drying | Example: Water boiling |

---

### Q3. Why does ice at 0°C cause more cooling than water at 0°C?

**Answer:**
- Ice at 0°C absorbs latent heat of fusion to convert to water at 0°C
- This additional heat absorption causes more cooling
- Water at 0°C does not absorb latent heat
- Latent heat of fusion of ice = 336 kJ/kg

---

### Q4. Convert 25°C to Kelvin and Fahrenheit.

**Answer:**
- K = °C + 273.15 = 25 + 273.15 = **298.15 K**
- °F = (9/5 × °C) + 32 = (9/5 × 25) + 32 = 45 + 32 = **77°F**

---

### Q5. Why do we see water droplets on the outer surface of a cold glass?

**Answer:**
- Water vapor in air comes in contact with cold glass surface
- Loses heat and condenses into water droplets
- This is **condensation**
- Droplets form on the outer surface

---

### Q6. At what temperature does water boil on Mount Everest? Why?

**Answer:**
- Water boils at approximately **70°C** on Mount Everest
- Atmospheric pressure is very low at high altitude (~0.6 atm)
- Boiling point decreases with decrease in pressure
- Less energy needed to overcome atmospheric pressure

---

### Q7. What is meant by latent heat? Give its types.

**Answer:**
- **Latent heat**: Heat energy absorbed or released during change of state without change in temperature
- **Latent heat of fusion**: Heat absorbed during melting (solid → liquid)
- **Latent heat of vaporization**: Heat absorbed during boiling (liquid → gas)
- Latent heat of fusion of ice = 336 kJ/kg
- Latent heat of vaporization of water = 2260 kJ/kg

---

### Q8. What is the SI unit of temperature? What is absolute zero?

**Answer:**
- SI unit of temperature: **Kelvin (K)**
- **Absolute zero**: 0 K = -273.15°C
- Lowest possible temperature
- At this temperature, particles have minimum possible kinetic energy
- Motion of particles theoretically stops at absolute zero

### Q9. Why does a sponge have air in it but is still called a solid?

**Answer:**
- Sponge has definite shape and definite volume
- It has small holes (spaces) in which air is trapped
- Air is compressible but the sponge material is not
- When we squeeze, we compress the air in the holes, not the solid material
- Therefore sponge is a solid with trapped air

---

### Q10. Explain why:
(a) A gas fills the entire container
(b) A gas is highly compressible

**Answer:**
- **(a)** Gas particles move freely at very high speeds in all directions
  - Very weak forces of attraction between particles
  - Particles spread out to fill entire container
- **(b)** Large spaces exist between gas particles
  - When pressure is applied, particles are pushed closer together
  - Gas can be compressed to a very small volume

---

### Q11. Which has more energy: steam at 100°C or water at 100°C? Why?

**Answer:**
- **Steam at 100°C** has more energy
- Steam contains additional latent heat of vaporization (2260 kJ/kg)
- Water at 100°C does not contain this latent heat
- Steam causes more severe burns because it releases this latent heat on condensation

---

### Q12. What is sublimation? Give two examples.

**Answer:**
- **Sublimation**: Direct change of state from solid to gas without becoming liquid
- Examples:
  1. Camphor (kapur) sublimes on heating
  2. Naphthalene balls sublime over time
  3. Dry ice (solid CO₂) sublimes at room temperature
  4. Ammonium chloride sublimes on heating
