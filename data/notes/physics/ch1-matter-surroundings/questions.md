## Exercise 1.1 — Q1

### Q1. Convert the following temperatures to the Kelvin scale:
(a) 25°C
(b) 373°C

**Answer:**
- K = °C + 273
- (a) 25°C = 25 + 273 = **298 K**
- (b) 373°C = 373 + 273 = **646 K**

## Exercise 1.1 — Q2

### Q2. Give reasons:
(a) A wooden table should be called a solid.
(b) We can smell perfume sitting several metres away.
(c) A pillow or a sponge fills with air when squeezed.
(d) A gas fills the vessel completely in which it is kept.

**Answer:**
- **(a)** A wooden table has a definite shape and definite volume → it is a solid
- **(b)** Perfume evaporates and its particles spread in air (diffusion) → we can smell it from far
- **(c)** Pillows and sponges have air trapped in the spaces between their particles → squeezing reduces the spaces and compresses the air
- **(d)** Gas particles move freely and randomly at high speed → they fill the entire container

## Exercise 1.1 — Q3

### Q3. Ice at 273 K produces more cooling effect than water at 273 K. Why?

**Answer:**
- Ice at 273 K must first absorb latent heat of fusion to convert to water at 273 K
- This absorption of heat causes more cooling than water at 273 K
- Water at 273 K does not absorb latent heat
- Therefore ice produces more cooling effect

## Exercise 1.1 — Q4

### Q4. Why do we see water droplets on the outer surface of a glass tumbler containing ice cold water?

**Answer:**
- Water vapor in surrounding air comes in contact with the cold surface of the glass
- The water vapor loses heat and condenses to form water droplets
- This process is called condensation
- The droplets form on the outer surface because that is where condensation occurs

## Exercise 1.1 — Q5

### Q5. At what temperature does water boil on Mount Everest?

**Answer:**
- Mount Everest has very low atmospheric pressure (about 0.6 atm)
- Boiling point of water decreases with decrease in pressure
- Water boils at approximately **70°C** on Mount Everest
- This is because at low pressure, water molecules need less energy to escape

## Exercise 1.1 — Q6

### Q6. Why does the temperature of a substance remain constant during its melting point or boiling point?

**Answer:**
- During melting or boiling, the heat energy absorbed is used to overcome the forces of attraction between particles
- This energy is called latent heat
- The temperature does not increase until all the substance has changed state
- Temperature remains constant until the entire substance has melted or boiled

## Exercise 1.1 — Q7

### Q7. Arrange the following in the order of increasing forces of attraction: water, sugar, oxygen.

**Answer:**
- Oxygen (gas) < Water (liquid) < Sugar (solid)
- Forces of attraction increase as: gas < liquid < solid
- Gas particles are far apart (weakest attraction)
- Liquid particles are close but can move (moderate attraction)
- Sugar particles are tightly packed in fixed positions (strongest attraction)

## Exercise 1.1 — Q8

### Q8. What is the physical state of water at:
(a) 25°C
(b) 0°C
(c) 100°C

**Answer:**
- **(a) 25°C**: Liquid (room temperature water)
- **(b) 0°C**: Both solid and liquid (ice and water coexist at melting/freezing point)
- **(c) 100°C**: Both liquid and gas (water and steam coexist at boiling/condensation point)

## Exercise 1.1 — Q9

### Q9. Why is water at 4°C more dense than water at 0°C?

**Answer:**
- At 4°C, water molecules are most closely packed
- As water cools below 4°C, molecules begin to spread apart (anomalous expansion)
- At 0°C, ice has open structure with more spaces between molecules
- Therefore water at 4°C is denser than water at 0°C
- This is why ice floats on water

## Exercise 1.1 — Q10

### Q10. Suggest two methods to liquefy a gas.

**Answer:**
1. **By increasing pressure**: Increasing pressure forces gas particles closer together → gas liquefies
2. **By decreasing temperature**: Reducing temperature reduces kinetic energy of particles → gas liquefies
- LPG (liquefied petroleum gas) is stored by applying high pressure at room temperature

## Exercise 1.1 — Q11

### Q11. Give reasons:
(a) Naphthalene balls disappear with time without leaving any solid.
(b) We can get the smell of perfume sitting several metres away.

**Answer:**
- **(a)** Naphthalene undergoes **sublimation** — changes directly from solid to gas without becoming liquid
  - The solid naphthalene particles gain energy and escape directly into air as gas
- **(b)** Perfume particles undergo **evaporation** and **diffusion**
  - Liquid perfume evaporates into gas
  - Gas particles spread uniformly in air by diffusion
  - Reaches our nose from several metres away

## Exercise 1.1 — Q12

### Q12. Convert:
(a) 373.15 K to °C
(b) 173.15 K to °C
(c) 293 K to °C

**Answer:**
- °C = K - 273.15
- **(a)** 373.15 - 273.15 = **100°C**
- **(b)** 173.15 - 273.15 = **-100°C**
- **(c)** 293 - 273 = **20°C**
