## Exercise 7.1 - Q1

### Q1. An object has moved through a distance. Can it have zero displacement? If so, support your answer with an example.

**Answer:**
- **Yes**, an object can have zero displacement even after moving through a distance
- If the initial and final positions are the same, displacement is zero
- **Example**: A person moves 3 km east and then 3 km west
  - Total distance = 6 km
  - Displacement = 3 - 3 = **0 km** (back to starting point)

## Exercise 7.1 - Q2

### Q2. A farmer moves along the boundary of a square field of side 10 m in 40 s. What will be the magnitude of displacement of the farmer at the end of 2 minutes 20 seconds?

**Answer:**
- Side of square = 10 m, perimeter = 40 m
- Time for one round = 40 s
- Total time = 2 min 20 s = 140 s
- Number of rounds = 140/40 = 3.5 rounds
- After 3 complete rounds, farmer is at starting point
- After 0.5 more round (20 m), farmer is at the diagonally opposite corner
- Displacement = diagonal of square = √(10² + 10²) = √200 = **10√2 m ≈ 14.1 m**

## Exercise 7.1 - Q3

### Q3. Under what condition(s) is the magnitude of average velocity of an object equal to its average speed?

**Answer:**
- Average speed = Total distance / Total time
- Average velocity = Total displacement / Total time
- These are equal when **distance = displacement**
- This happens when the object moves in a **straight line without changing direction**
- Or when the motion is in one direction only (no turning back)

## Exercise 7.1 - Q4

### Q4. What does the odometer of an automobile measure?

**Answer:**
- The **odometer** measures the **total distance traveled** by the automobile
- It does not measure displacement
- It records the cumulative distance covered regardless of direction

## Exercise 7.1 - Q5

### Q5. What does the path of an object look like when it is in uniform motion?

**Answer:**
- In uniform motion, the distance-time graph is a **straight line passing through origin**
- This means the object covers equal distances in equal intervals of time
- The slope of the line = speed (constant)
- The path can be straight or curved, but speed is constant

## Exercise 7.1 - Q6

### Q6. During an experiment, a signal from a spaceship reached the ground station in 5 minutes. What was the distance of the spaceship from the ground station? (Speed of signal = 3 × 10⁸ m/s)

**Answer:**
- Time = 5 minutes = 5 × 60 = 300 seconds
- Speed = 3 × 10⁸ m/s
- Distance = Speed × Time
- Distance = 3 × 10⁸ × 300 = 9 × 10¹⁰ m = **9 × 10⁷ km**

## Exercise 7.2 - Q1

### Q1. When can we say that distance and displacement are the same?

**Answer:**
- Distance and displacement are the same when the motion is in a **straight line without changing direction**
- In this case, the actual path length (distance) equals the shortest path (displacement)
- This occurs when there is no change in direction of motion

## Exercise 7.2 - Q2

### Q2. A bus decreases its speed from 80 km/h to 60 km/h in 5 s. Find the acceleration of the bus.

**Answer:**
- Initial velocity (u) = 80 km/h = 80 × 5/18 = 22.22 m/s
- Final velocity (v) = 60 km/h = 60 × 5/18 = 16.67 m/s
- Time (t) = 5 s
- Acceleration (a) = (v - u)/t = (16.67 - 22.22)/5 = -5.55/5 = **-1.11 m/s²**
- Negative sign indicates retardation (deceleration)

## Exercise 7.2 - Q3

### Q3. A train starting from a railway station and moving with uniform acceleration attains a speed of 40 km/h in 10 minutes. Find the acceleration.

**Answer:**
- Initial velocity (u) = 0 (starting from rest)
- Final velocity (v) = 40 km/h = 40 × 5/18 = 11.11 m/s
- Time (t) = 10 minutes = 600 s
- Acceleration (a) = (v - u)/t = (11.11 - 0)/600 = **0.0185 m/s²**

## Exercise 7.2 - Q4

### Q4. A body moves along a straight line with a constant acceleration of 5 m/s². If its initial velocity is 2 m/s, find the distance covered in 10 seconds.

**Answer:**
- u = 2 m/s, a = 5 m/s², t = 10 s
- Using s = ut + ½at²
- s = 2 × 10 + ½ × 5 × 10²
- s = 20 + ½ × 5 × 100
- s = 20 + 250 = **270 m**

### Q5. The speed-time graph of a car is shown. Find the distance covered by the car in 10 seconds.

**Answer:**
- Distance covered = Area under speed-time graph
- If graph is a straight line (uniform acceleration), area = ½ × base × height (for triangle) or area of trapezium
- Need to read values from graph

## Exercise 7.3 - Q1

### Q1. When a satellite moves in a circular orbit around the Earth with uniform speed, is its motion uniform or non-uniform? Give reasons.

**Answer:**
- Speed is constant but **velocity is not constant** (direction changes continuously)
- Therefore, the motion is **non-uniform** (accelerated motion)
- Acceleration is always directed towards the center of the Earth (centripetal acceleration)
- The direction of motion changes at every point in the circular orbit

## Exercise 7.3 - Q2

### Q2. The distance between two celestial bodies is very large. Which unit is suitable to express this distance?

**Answer:**
- **Light year** is the most suitable unit
- 1 light year = Distance traveled by light in 1 year = 9.46 × 10¹⁵ m
- Other suitable units: **parsec** (3.08 × 10¹⁶ m), **astronomical unit** (1 AU = 1.496 × 10¹¹ m)

## In-Text Questions

### Q1. (Page 100) What is the difference between distance and displacement?

**Answer:**
- Distance: Total path length, scalar, always positive
- Displacement: Shortest distance from initial to final position, vector, can be zero
- Example: Going around a circular track → distance = circumference, displacement = 0

### Q2. (Page 103) What does the slope of a distance-time graph represent?

**Answer:**
- The slope of a distance-time graph represents **speed**
- Steeper slope = higher speed
- Straight line = uniform speed
- Curved line = non-uniform speed

### Q3. (Page 104) What does the slope of a velocity-time graph represent?

**Answer:**
- The slope of a velocity-time graph represents **acceleration**
- Steeper slope = higher acceleration
- Straight line = uniform acceleration
- Curved line = non-uniform acceleration
