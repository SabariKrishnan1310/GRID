# Motion - Detailed Concepts

## 7.1 Introduction to Motion

- **Motion**: An object is said to be in motion if its position changes with respect to time and its surroundings
  - Motion is relative - depends on the observer's frame of reference
  - An object that appears stationary to one observer may be in motion to another
  - Example: A passenger in a moving train appears stationary to another passenger but is in motion to a person standing outside

- **Reference point**: A fixed point used to determine the position of an object
  - Any object can be described as being in motion or at rest depending on the reference point chosen
  - For convenience, we often take the surface of the Earth as the reference point

## 7.2 Distance and Displacement

- **Distance**: The total path length covered by an object during its motion
  - Scalar quantity - has only magnitude, no direction
  - Always positive (or zero)
  - SI unit: metre (m)
  - Depends on the actual path taken
  - Example: A car travels 5 km towards east, then 3 km towards west
  - Total distance = 5 + 3 = 8 km

- **Displacement**: The shortest straight-line distance from the initial position to the final position of an object
  - Vector quantity - has both magnitude and direction
  - Can be positive, negative, or zero
  - SI unit: metre (m)
  - Depends only on initial and final positions, not on the path taken
  - Example: Same car → displacement = 5 - 3 = 2 km towards east

- **Relationship**: Distance ≥ Displacement (distance is always greater than or equal to displacement)
  - Distance = Displacement only when motion is in a straight line without changing direction

## 7.3 Speed and Velocity

- **Speed**: The distance covered by an object per unit time
  - Speed = Distance / Time
  - Scalar quantity - has only magnitude
  - SI unit: m/s (also km/h, cm/s)
  - Conversion: 1 m/s = 3.6 km/h
  - **Uniform speed**: An object covers equal distances in equal intervals of time
  - **Non-uniform speed**: An object covers unequal distances in equal intervals of time
  - **Average speed**: Total distance covered / Total time taken

- **Velocity**: The displacement of an object per unit time
  - Velocity = Displacement / Time = Δs / Δt
  - Vector quantity - has both magnitude and direction
  - SI unit: m/s
  - **Uniform velocity**: An object undergoes equal displacements in equal intervals of time
  - **Non-uniform velocity**: An object undergoes unequal displacements in equal intervals of time
  - **Average velocity**: Total displacement / Total time

- **Difference between speed and velocity**:
  - Speed is scalar; velocity is vector
  - Speed is always positive; velocity can be positive, negative, or zero
  - Speed depends on distance; velocity depends on displacement

## 7.4 Acceleration

- **Acceleration**: The rate of change of velocity of an object
  - Acceleration = (Final velocity - Initial velocity) / Time
  - a = (v - u) / t
  - SI unit: m/s²
  - Vector quantity
  - **Uniform acceleration**: Equal change in velocity in equal intervals of time
  - **Non-uniform acceleration**: Unequal change in velocity in equal intervals of time
  - **Retardation/Deceleration**: Negative acceleration (velocity decreases with time)

- **Example**: A car starting from rest attains a velocity of 20 m/s in 4 seconds
  - Acceleration = (20 - 0) / 4 = 5 m/s²

## 7.5 Equations of Motion

For an object moving with **uniform acceleration**:

### First Equation of Motion: v = u + at
- Derived from the definition of acceleration: a = (v - u)/t
- Rearranging: v = u + at
- **v** = final velocity, **u** = initial velocity, **a** = acceleration, **t** = time
- Relates velocity, acceleration, and time

### Second Equation of Motion: s = ut + ½at²
- Average velocity = (u + v)/2
- Displacement = Average velocity × time = ½(u + v)t
- Substituting v = u + at: s = ½(u + u + at)t = ½(2u + at)t
- s = ut + ½at²
- **s** = displacement
- Relates displacement, initial velocity, acceleration, and time

### Third Equation of Motion: v² = u² + 2as
- From first equation: t = (v - u)/a
- Substituting in second equation: s = u(v - u)/a + ½a(v - u)²/a²
- Simplifying: v² = u² + 2as
- Relates velocity, acceleration, and displacement

## 7.6 Graphical Representation of Motion

### Distance-Time Graph
- **Uniform speed**: Straight line passing through origin
  - Slope of the line = speed
  - Steeper slope = higher speed
- **Non-uniform speed**: Curved line
  - Slope at any point = instantaneous speed at that point
- **Object at rest**: Straight line parallel to time axis
  - Slope = 0 (speed = 0)

### Velocity-Time Graph
- **Uniform acceleration**: Straight line with non-zero slope
  - Slope of the line = acceleration
  - Steeper slope = higher acceleration
- **Non-uniform acceleration**: Curved line
  - Slope at any point = instantaneous acceleration
- **Uniform velocity (zero acceleration)**: Straight line parallel to time axis
  - Slope = 0 (acceleration = 0)
- **Distance covered** = Area under the velocity-time graph
  - For uniform acceleration: area of trapezium = ½(u + v)t

## 7.7 Uniform Circular Motion

- **Uniform circular motion**: Motion of an object in a circular path with constant speed
  - Speed remains constant but velocity changes continuously (direction changes at every point)
  - Acceleration is always directed towards the center of the circle (centripetal acceleration)
  - This acceleration is caused by the centripetal force

- **Time period (T)**: The time taken by an object to complete one revolution
  - T = 2πr / v
  - r = radius of the circular path
  - v = speed of the object

- **Frequency (f)**: The number of revolutions completed per second
  - f = 1/T
  - SI unit: Hertz (Hz)

- **Speed in uniform circular motion**: v = 2πr / T = 2πrf
  - Even though speed is constant, velocity is not constant because direction changes
  - Therefore, the object is accelerated (centripetal acceleration)
  - Example: The Moon revolves around the Earth with approximately uniform speed
  - Example: The second hand of a clock moves in a circular path with uniform speed

- **Examples of uniform circular motion**:
  - An athlete running on a circular track at constant speed
  - The motion of the Earth around the Sun
  - The motion of a satellite around a planet
  - A stone tied to a thread and whirled in a horizontal circle
