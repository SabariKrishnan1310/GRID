## Exercise 3.1 — Q1

### Q1. In a reaction, 5.3 g of sodium carbonate reacted with 6 g of acetic acid. The products were 2.2 g of carbon dioxide, 0.9 g of water and 8.2 g of sodium acetate. Show that these observations are in agreement with the Law of Conservation of Mass.

**Answer:**
- Mass of reactants = 5.3 + 6 = **11.3 g**
- Mass of products = 2.2 + 0.9 + 8.2 = **11.3 g**
- Mass of reactants = Mass of products
- Therefore, the observations are in agreement with the **Law of Conservation of Mass**

## Exercise 3.1 — Q2

### Q2. Hydrogen and oxygen combine in the ratio of 1:8 by mass to form water. What mass of oxygen gas would be required to react completely with 3 g of hydrogen gas?

**Answer:**
- Ratio of H:O by mass = 1:8
- 1 g hydrogen reacts with 8 g oxygen
- 3 g hydrogen reacts with 3 × 8 = **24 g oxygen**

## Exercise 3.1 — Q3

### Q3. Which postulate of Dalton's atomic theory is the result of the law of conservation of mass?

**Answer:**
- Postulate: "Atoms are indivisible particles, which cannot be created or destroyed in a chemical reaction"
- This directly follows from the **Law of Conservation of Mass**
- Mass is conserved because atoms are neither created nor destroyed

## Exercise 3.1 — Q4

### Q4. Which postulate of Dalton's atomic theory explains the law of constant proportions?

**Answer:**
- Postulate: "The relative number and kinds of atoms are constant in a given compound"
- This explains why a compound always has the same elements in the same proportion by mass

## Exercise 3.2 — Q1

### Q1. Define the term atomic mass unit.

**Answer:**
- **Atomic mass unit (u)**: The unit used to express atomic and molecular masses
- 1 u = 1/12 of the mass of one carbon-12 atom
- 1 u = 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ kg
- Example: Hydrogen atom has a mass of 1 u

## Exercise 3.2 — Q2

### Q2. Why is it not possible to see an atom with naked eyes?

**Answer:**
- Atoms are extremely small — radius of the order of 10⁻¹⁰ m (1 Å)
- Even the largest atoms (like francium) have a radius of only about 3.6 × 10⁻¹⁰ m
- This is far too small to be visible to the naked eye
- Cannot be seen even with an optical microscope

## Exercise 3.3 — Q1

### Q1. How do atoms exist?

**Answer:**
- Atoms exist as **molecules** (when they combine)
- Atoms exist as **ions** (when they gain or lose electrons)
- Atoms exist as **isolated neutral atoms** (for noble gases like He, Ne, Ar)
- Atoms of metals exist as a **cluster of atoms** (not as individual molecules)

## Exercise 3.5 — Q1

### Q1. Calculate the molecular masses of:
(a) H₂, O₂, Cl₂, CO₂, CH₄, C₂H₅OH, NH₃, CH₃OH

**Answer:**
- H₂ = 2(1) = **2 u**
- O₂ = 2(16) = **32 u**
- Cl₂ = 2(35.5) = **71 u**
- CO₂ = 12 + 2(16) = **44 u**
- CH₄ = 12 + 4(1) = **16 u**
- C₂H₅OH = 2(12) + 6(1) + 16 = **46 u**
- NH₃ = 14 + 3(1) = **17 u**
- CH₃OH = 12 + 4(1) + 16 = **32 u**

## Exercise 3.6 — Q1

### Q1. If one mole of carbon atoms weighs 12 g, what is the mass of one atom of carbon?

**Answer:**
- 1 mole of carbon = 6.022 × 10²³ atoms = 12 g
- Mass of 1 atom = 12 / (6.022 × 10²³) = **1.99 × 10⁻²³ g**
- Or **12 u** (atomic mass unit)

## Exercise 3.6 — Q2

### Q2. Which has more number of atoms, 10 g of nitrogen or 10 g of ammonia?

**Answer:**
- **10 g of nitrogen (N₂)**:
  - Molecular mass of N₂ = 28 u
  - Moles = 10/28 = 0.357 mol
  - Number of atoms = 0.357 × 6.022 × 10²³ × 2 = **4.3 × 10²³ atoms**

- **10 g of ammonia (NH₃)**:
  - Molecular mass of NH₃ = 17 u
  - Moles = 10/17 = 0.588 mol
  - Number of atoms = 0.588 × 6.022 × 10²³ × 4 = **1.42 × 10²⁴ atoms**

- **10 g of ammonia has more atoms** (1.42 × 10²⁴ > 4.3 × 10²³)
