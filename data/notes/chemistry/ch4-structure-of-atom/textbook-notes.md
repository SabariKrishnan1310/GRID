## 4.1 Charged Particles in Matter

- **Atom** consists of charged particles (subatomic particles)
- **J.J. Thomson (1897)** discovered electron - cathode ray experiment
- **Goldstein (1886)** discovered anode rays (canal rays) - positively charged particles
- **Chadwick (1932)** discovered neutron - neutral particle, no charge

**Thomson's Model:**
- Atom is neutral overall - equal number of positive and negative charges
- Atom is a sphere of uniform positive charge with electrons embedded like seeds in a watermelon
- Model called "plum pudding model"

## 4.2 Thomson's Atomic Model

- Atom is uniformly positively charged sphere
- Electrons distributed uniformly within the sphere
- Total positive charge = total negative charge
- Model failed to explain alpha particle scattering results

## 4.3 Rutherford's Atomic Model

- **Rutherford (1911)** - alpha particle scattering experiment (Geiger-Marsden)
- **Alpha particles**: Helium nuclei (He²⁺), mass number 4, charge +2e
- **Experiment**: Alpha particles fired at thin gold foil (0.00004 cm thick)
  - Most passed straight through
  - Some deflected by small angles
  - Very few (1 in 20,000) deflected by more than 90°

**Observations:**
1. Most alpha particles passed undeflected → atom is mostly empty space
2. Some deflected by small angles → positive charge concentrated in small region
3. Very few deflected by large angles → dense, positively charged nucleus

**Conclusions:**
- **Nucleus**: Small, dense, positively charged center
- Nucleus contains **protons** and **neutrons**
- Nucleus volume is very small compared to atom → atom is mostly empty space
- Electrons revolve around nucleus at high speed

**Drawbacks of Rutherford's Model:**
- Accelerating charged particle loses energy continuously
- Electron should spiral into nucleus → atom should collapse
- Does not explain atomic spectra (line spectra of elements)

## 4.4 Bohr's Model of Atom

- **Bohr (1913)** modified Rutherford's model to explain line spectra
- **Postulates:**
  1. Electrons orbit nucleus in fixed circular paths called **shells** (energy levels)
  2. Each shell has fixed energy → called **quantized energy levels**
  3. Shells designated by n = 1, 2, 3, 4... or K, L, M, N...
  4. Electron does not radiate energy while in a stationary orbit
  5. Energy emitted/absorbed only when electron jumps between orbits
  6. Energy absorbed when jumping from lower to higher orbit
  7. Energy emitted when jumping from higher to lower orbit
  8. **Bohr-Bury scheme**: Maximum electrons in a shell = 2n²

**Electron Distribution:**
- K shell (n=1): maximum 2 electrons
- L shell (n=2): maximum 8 electrons
- M shell (n=3): maximum 18 electrons
- Outermost shell: maximum 8 electrons (octet rule)
- Inner shells must be filled before electrons occupy outer shells

## 4.5 Neutrons

- **Chadwick (1932)** discovered neutron
- Neutron has **no charge**, mass approximately equal to proton (1.675 × 10⁻²⁷ kg)
- Present in nucleus of all atoms (except hydrogen)
- **Isotopes**: Atoms of same element with different number of neutrons
  - Same atomic number, different mass number
  - Example: Hydrogen-1 (0 neutrons), Hydrogen-2 (1 neutron), Hydrogen-3 (2 neutrons)
- **Isobars**: Atoms of different elements with same mass number but different atomic numbers
  - Example: Argon-40, Calcium-40, Potassium-40 - all have mass number 40

## 4.6 Atomic Number and Mass Number

- **Atomic number (Z)**: Number of protons in nucleus
  - Defines the element (all atoms of an element have same Z)
- **Mass number (A)**: Total number of protons + neutrons in nucleus
  - A = Z + N (where N = number of neutrons)
- **Nucleons**: Protons and neutrons collectively
- **Nuclide notation**: ᴬ_Z X where X is element symbol

## 4.8 Valency

- **Valency**: Combining capacity of an atom (number of electrons needed to complete octet)
- Elements with complete outer shell (noble gases) are inert - valency 0
- Elements with 1-3 electrons in outer shell lose electrons to attain stability
- Elements with 5-7 electrons in outer shell gain electrons to attain stability
- Elements in 2nd shell have maximum valency of 4
- **Duplet rule**: Hydrogen, Helium, Lithium attain stability with 2 electrons in K shell

## 4.9 Isotopes and Isobars

- **Isotopes**: Same Z, different A (different neutron count)
  - Same chemical properties (same electron configuration)
  - Different physical properties (different atomic mass)
  - Examples: ¹H, ²H (D), ³H (T); ¹²C, ¹³C, ¹⁴C; ³⁵Cl, ³⁷Cl
  - Carbon-14 used in radiocarbon dating
- **Isobars**: Different Z, same A
  - Different chemical properties (different elements)
  - Same mass number
  - Example: ⁴⁰₁₈Ar, ⁴⁰₁₉K, ⁴⁰₂₀Ca

## Quick Formulas

- **A = Z + N** (Mass number = Protons + Neutrons)
- **Maximum electrons in shell n** = 2n²
- **Number of neutrons** = A − Z
- **Number of electrons** = Z (for neutral atom)
