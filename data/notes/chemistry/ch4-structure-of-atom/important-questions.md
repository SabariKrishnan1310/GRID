# Structure of the Atom — Formulas & Important Questions

## Key Formulas

| # | Formula | Variables | When to Use |
|---|---------|-----------|-------------|
| 1 | $Z = p = e$ (neutral atom) | $Z$ = atomic number, $p$ = protons, $e$ = electrons | Find atomic number from proton count |
| 2 | $A = p + n$ | $A$ = mass number, $p$ = protons, $n$ = neutrons | Find mass number |
| 3 | $n = A - Z$ | $n$ = neutrons, $A$ = mass number, $Z$ = atomic number | Find number of neutrons |
| 4 | Max electrons in shell = $2n^2$ | $n$ = shell number | Find electron capacity of shell |
| 5 | ${}_{Z}^{A}X$ | $X$ = element symbol, $A$ = mass number, $Z$ = atomic number | Symbolic notation of element |

## Important Questions (Board Exam Frequent)

### Type 1: Thomson's Model

**Q: Describe Thomson's model of atom. What are its limitations?**

**Answer:**

Thomson proposed that:
- Atom is a **positively charged sphere** of radius $10^{-10}$ m
- Positive charge is **uniformly distributed** throughout the sphere
- **Electrons are embedded** in this sphere like seeds in a watermelon
- The total positive charge equals the total negative charge, making atom electrically neutral

**Limitations:**
- Could not explain **Rutherford's alpha particle scattering experiment**
- Did not describe how electrons are arranged within the atom
- Could not explain the **line spectra** of atoms

---

### Type 2: Rutherford's Experiment

**Q: Describe Rutherford's alpha particle scattering experiment. What did it establish?**

**Answer:**

**Experiment:**
- A beam of **positively charged alpha particles** (${}_{2}^{4}He$) was directed at a **thin gold foil**
- A circular **fluorescent zinc sulphide screen** was placed around the foil to detect deflected particles

**Observations:**
- **Most alpha particles** passed straight through (atom is mostly empty space)
- **Some** were deflected by small angles (positive charge is concentrated in a small region)
- **Very few** bounced back at 180 degrees (entire positive charge is in a tiny, dense nucleus)

**Conclusions:**
- Atom has a **dense, positively charged nucleus** at the centre
- Nucleus is extremely small ($10^{-15}$ m) compared to atom ($10^{-10}$ m)
- Electrons revolve around the nucleus at large distances

**Limitations:**
- Could not explain **stability** of atom (electrons should lose energy and spiral into nucleus)
- Could not explain **line spectra** of atoms

### Type 3: Bohr's Model

**Q: What are the main features of Bohr's model of atom?**

**Answer:**

Bohr's postulates:
1. Electrons revolve in **fixed circular orbits** called **shells** or **energy levels**
2. Each orbit has a **fixed energy** and **fixed radius**
3. Electrons in these orbits do **not radiate energy** (this explains atomic stability)
4. Each orbit is numbered: K ($n=1$), L ($n=2$), M ($n=3$), N ($n=4$)
5. Energy increases away from nucleus: K < L < M < N
6. Electrons jump between orbits by absorbing/emitting energy
7. Angular momentum is quantized: $mvr = \frac{nh}{2\pi}$

### Type 6: Valency

**Q: Define valency. How is it determined from electronic configuration?**

**Answer:**

**Valency** is the combining capacity of an atom, determined by the number of electrons in the outermost shell.

**Rules:**
- **1-4 electrons** in outermost shell: valency = number of electrons (atom loses them)
- **5-7 electrons** in outermost shell: valency = 8 - number of electrons (atom gains electrons)
- **8 electrons** in outermost shell: valency = 0 (noble gas, stable)

**Examples:**
- Sodium (2, 8, 1): loses 1 electron → valency = 1
- Chlorine (2, 8, 7): gains 1 electron → valency = 1
- Carbon (2, 4): shares 4 electrons → valency = 4
- Neon (2, 8): complete octet → valency = 0

### Type 7: Numerical Problems

**Q: An atom has atomic number 17 and mass number 35. Find the number of protons, neutrons, and electrons.**

**Solution:**

Given: $Z = 17$, $A = 35$

$$
\text{Number of protons} = Z = 17
$$

$$
\text{Number of neutrons} = A - Z = 35 - 17 = 18
$$

$$
\text{Number of electrons} = Z = 17 \text{ (neutral atom)}
$$

**Answer:** 17 protons, 18 neutrons, 17 electrons. (This is Chlorine-35)

---

**Q: The atomic number and mass number of an element are 12 and 24 respectively. Write its notation and find the number of neutrons.**

**Solution:**

$$
{}_{Z}^{A}X = {}_{12}^{24}Mg
$$

$$
n = A - Z = 24 - 12 = 12
$$

**Answer:** The element is Magnesium (${}_{12}^{24}Mg$) with 12 neutrons.
