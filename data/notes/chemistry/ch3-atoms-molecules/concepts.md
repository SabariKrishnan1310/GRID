# Atoms and Molecules - Detailed Concepts

## 3.1 Laws of Chemical Combination

- **Law of Conservation of Mass**: Total mass of reactants equals total mass of products in a chemical reaction
  - Discovered by **Antoine Lavoisier** in 1774
  - Mass is neither created nor destroyed during a chemical reaction
  - Only rearrangement of atoms takes place
  - Example: When 34 g of silver nitrate reacts with 17 g of sodium chloride, it forms 143.5 g of silver chloride and 17 g of sodium nitrate
  - Total reactants (34 + 17 = 51 g) = Total products (143.5 + 17 = 51 g)

- **Law of Constant Proportions**: A chemical compound always contains the same elements combined in the same proportion by mass
  - Discovered by **Joseph Proust** in 1799
  - The composition of a pure compound is always the same regardless of its source or method of preparation
  - Example: Water always contains hydrogen and oxygen in the ratio 1:8 by mass
  - Any sample of water from any source (river, sea, well) contains 11.2% hydrogen and 88.8% oxygen by mass

- **Dalton's Atomic Theory**: Proposed by John Dalton to explain the laws of chemical combination
  - All matter is made of extremely small particles called atoms
  - Atoms are indivisible and cannot be created or destroyed in a chemical reaction
  - Atoms of a given element are identical in mass and chemical properties
  - Atoms of different elements have different masses and chemical properties
  - Atoms combine in the ratio of small whole numbers to form compounds
  - The relative number and kinds of atoms are constant in a given compound

## 3.2 What is an Atom?

- **Atom**: The smallest particle of an element that retains its identity during chemical reactions
  - Atoms are extremely small - radius is of the order of 10⁻¹⁰ m (1 angstrom, 1 Å)
  - Size of hydrogen atom is approximately 10⁻¹¹ m
  - Largest atoms are those of francium and radium with a radius of about 3.6 × 10⁻¹⁰ m
  - Atoms are so small that they cannot be seen even with an optical microscope

- **Symbols**: Each element is represented by a symbol
  - First letter of the element name is written in capital
  - Second letter (if any) is written in small
  - Example: Carbon → C, Calcium → Ca, Sodium → Na
  - Some symbols are derived from Latin names: Fe (Iron from Ferrum), Ag (Silver from Argentum), Au (Gold from Aurum)

- **Atomicity**: Number of atoms in one molecule of an element or compound
  - Monoatomic: Helium (He), Neon (Ne), Argon (Ar)
  - Diatomic: Hydrogen (H₂), Oxygen (O₂), Nitrogen (N₂), Chlorine (Cl₂)
  - Triatomic: Ozone (O₃)
  - Tetraatomic: Phosphorus (P₄)
  - Polyatomic: Sulphur (S₈)

## 3.3 What is a Molecule?

- **Molecule**: A group of two or more atoms chemically bonded together
  - Molecules are the smallest unit of a substance that can exist independently
  - Molecules are larger than atoms but still extremely small

- **Molecules of Elements**: Molecules made of atoms of the same element
  - Diatomic molecules: H₂, O₂, N₂, Cl₂, Br₂, I₂
  - Triatomic molecules: O₃ (ozone)
  - Polyatomic molecules: P₄, S₈

- **Molecules of Compounds**: Molecules made of atoms of different elements
  - Water (H₂O): 2 hydrogen atoms + 1 oxygen atom
  - Carbon dioxide (CO₂): 1 carbon atom + 2 oxygen atoms
  - Methane (CH₄): 1 carbon atom + 4 hydrogen atoms
  - Ammonia (NH₃): 1 nitrogen atom + 3 hydrogen atoms

## 3.4 Ions

- **Ion**: An atom or group of atoms that carries a positive or negative charge
  - Formed by gain or loss of electrons
  - Atoms are electrically neutral (equal number of protons and electrons)
  - When atoms gain or lose electrons, they become charged

- **Cation**: Positively charged ion formed by loss of electrons
  - Sodium atom (Na) loses 1 electron → Sodium ion (Na⁺)
  - Magnesium atom (Mg) loses 2 electrons → Magnesium ion (Mg²⁺)
  - Aluminium atom (Al) loses 3 electrons → Aluminium ion (Al³⁺)

- **Anion**: Negatively charged ion formed by gain of electrons
  - Chlorine atom (Cl) gains 1 electron → Chloride ion (Cl⁻)
  - Oxygen atom (O) gains 2 electrons → Oxide ion (O²⁻)
  - Nitrogen atom (N) gains 3 electrons → Nitride ion (N³⁻)

- **Polyatomic Ions**: Groups of atoms that carry a charge
  - Sulphate (SO₄²⁻): 1 sulphur + 4 oxygen atoms with charge -2
  - Carbonate (CO₃²⁻): 1 carbon + 3 oxygen atoms with charge -2
  - Hydroxide (OH⁻): 1 oxygen + 1 hydrogen atom with charge -1
  - Nitrate (NO₃⁻): 1 nitrogen + 3 oxygen atoms with charge -1
  - Ammonium (NH₄⁺): 1 nitrogen + 4 hydrogen atoms with charge +1

## 3.5 Writing Chemical Formulae

- **Chemical Formula**: Representation of a compound using symbols and subscripts
  - Tells which elements are present and how many atoms of each
  - Based on the valency (combining capacity) of elements

- **Rules for Writing Chemical Formulae**:
  1. Write the symbol of the element with positive valency (cation) first
  2. Write the symbol of the element with negative valency (anion) second
  3. Cross over the valencies: place the valency of cation as subscript of anion and vice versa
  4. Reduce the subscripts to the simplest whole number ratio

- **Examples of Chemical Formulae**:
  - Sodium chloride: Na⁺ (valency 1) + Cl⁻ (valency 1) → NaCl
  - Magnesium chloride: Mg²⁺ (valency 2) + Cl⁻ (valency 1) → MgCl₂
  - Aluminium chloride: Al³⁺ (valency 3) + Cl⁻ (valency 1) → AlCl₃
  - Calcium oxide: Ca²⁺ (valency 2) + O²⁻ (valency 2) → CaO
  - Aluminium oxide: Al³⁺ (valency 3) + O²⁻ (valency 2) → Al₂O₃
  - Sodium oxide: Na⁺ (valency 1) + O²⁻ (valency 2) → Na₂O

- **Formulae with Polyatomic Ions**:
  - Calcium hydroxide: Ca²⁺ + 2OH⁻ → Ca(OH)₂
  - Magnesium nitrate: Mg²⁺ + 2NO₃⁻ → Mg(NO₃)₂
  - Sodium sulphate: 2Na⁺ + SO₄²⁻ → Na₂SO₄
  - Calcium carbonate: Ca²⁺ + CO₃²⁻ → CaCO₃
  - Aluminium sulphate: 2Al³⁺ + 3SO₄²⁻ → Al₂(SO₄)₃

## 3.6 Molecular Mass and Formula Unit Mass

- **Molecular Mass**: Sum of atomic masses of all atoms present in one molecule of a substance
  - Calculated by adding up the atomic masses of all atoms in the chemical formula
  - Unit: unified atomic mass unit (u) or atomic mass unit (amu)
  - Example: Water (H₂O) = 2(1) + 16 = 18 u
  - Example: Carbon dioxide (CO₂) = 12 + 2(16) = 44 u
  - Example: Sulphuric acid (H₂SO₄) = 2(1) + 32 + 4(16) = 98 u

- **Formula Unit Mass**: Sum of atomic masses of all atoms in a formula unit of an ionic compound
  - Same calculation as molecular mass but applied to ionic compounds
  - Ionic compounds do not exist as molecules; they exist as formula units
  - Example: Sodium chloride (NaCl) = 23 + 35.5 = 58.5 u
  - Example: Calcium chloride (CaCl₂) = 40 + 2(35.5) = 111 u
  - Example: Aluminium oxide (Al₂O₃) = 2(27) + 3(16) = 102 u

- **Mole Concept**: One mole of any substance contains 6.022 × 10²³ particles (Avogadro's number)
  - 1 mole of any substance = its molecular mass in grams
  - Example: 1 mole of water = 18 g
  - Example: 1 mole of CO₂ = 44 g
  - Example: 1 mole of NaCl = 58.5 g
