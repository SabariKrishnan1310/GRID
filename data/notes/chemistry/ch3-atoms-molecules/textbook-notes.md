## 3.1 Laws of Chemical Combination

### Law of Conservation of Mass
- Mass is neither created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction
- Total mass of reactants = Total mass of products
- Proved by **Lavoisier** (1774)
- Example: 34 g AgNO₃ + 17 g NaCl → 143.5 g AgCl + 17 g NaNO₃; total reactants = 51 g = total products

### Law of Constant Proportions
- A chemical compound always contains the same elements in the same proportion by mass
- Proved by **Joseph Proust** (1799)
- Example: Water always contains hydrogen and oxygen in ratio 1:8 by mass
- A sample of pure water from any source (river, sea, well) will always contain 11.2% hydrogen and 88.8% oxygen by mass

### Dalton's Atomic Theory
- All matter is made of extremely small particles called atoms
- Atoms are indivisible and cannot be created or destroyed in a chemical reaction
- Atoms of a given element are identical in mass and chemical properties
- Atoms of different elements have different masses and chemical properties
- Atoms combine in the ratio of small whole numbers to form compounds
- The relative number and kinds of atoms are constant in a given compound

## 3.2 What is an Atom?

- Smallest particle of an element that retains its identity during chemical reactions
- Atoms are extremely small - radius = 10⁻¹⁰ m (1 Å)
- **Size**: Hydrogen atom smallest (radius ~10⁻¹¹ m); largest atoms are francium/radium (~3.6 × 10⁻¹⁰ m)
- Atoms are so small they cannot be seen even with optical microscope
- **Symbols**: First letter (capital) + second letter (small) if needed → Fe, Ca, Na

### Dalton's Modern Symbols
- Each element is represented by a symbol
- First letter is always capital; second letter (if any) is always small
- Symbols represent one atom of that element

## 3.3 What is a Molecule?

- A molecule is a group of two or more atoms chemically bonded together
- Smallest unit of a substance that can exist independently
- Molecules are also very small but larger than atoms

### Types of Molecules

**Molecules of Elements:**
- **Monoatomic**: He, Ne, Ar (single atoms)
- **Diatomic**: H₂, O₂, N₂, Cl₂, Br₂, I₂
- **Triatomic**: O₃ (ozone)
- **Polyatomic**: P₄, S₈

**Molecules of Compounds:**
- Formed by atoms of different elements
- Example: H₂O (2 hydrogen + 1 oxygen)
- Example: CO₂ (1 carbon + 2 oxygen)
- Example: NaCl (1 sodium + 1 chlorine)

### Atomicity
- Number of atoms constituting a molecule
- H₂ → atomicity = 2 (diatomic)
- O₃ → atomicity = 3 (triatomic)
- P₄ → atomicity = 4 (tetraatomic)
- S₈ → atomicity = 8 (polyatomic)

## 3.4 Ions

- An ion is an atom or group of atoms with a positive or negative charge
- **Cation**: Positive ion → formed by loss of electrons
  - Na → Na⁺ + e⁻ (sodium ion)
  - Ca → Ca²⁺ + 2e⁻ (calcium ion)
  - Al → Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ (aluminium ion)
- **Anion**: Negative ion → formed by gain of electrons
  - Cl + e⁻ → Cl⁻ (chloride ion)
  - O + 2e⁻ → O²⁻ (oxide ion)
- **Polyatomic ions**: Group of atoms with charge
  - SO₄²⁻ (sulphate), CO₃²⁻ (carbonate), OH⁻ (hydroxide), NO₃⁻ (nitrate), NH₄⁺ (ammonium)

## 3.5 Writing Chemical Formulae

- Chemical formula tells which elements and how many atoms of each are in a compound
- Based on **valency** (combining capacity of an element)

### Rules
1. Write the symbol of the element with positive valency first (cation)
2. Write the symbol of the element with negative valency second (anion)
3. Cross over the valencies as subscripts
4. Reduce subscripts to simplest ratio if possible

### Examples
- **NaCl**: Na⁺ (valency 1) + Cl⁻ (valency 1) → NaCl
- **MgCl₂**: Mg²⁺ (valency 2) + Cl⁻ (valency 1) → MgCl₂
- **AlCl₃**: Al³⁺ (valency 3) + Cl⁻ (valency 1) → AlCl₃
- **Al₂O₃**: Al³⁺ (valency 3) + O²⁻ (valency 2) → Al₂O₃
- **CaO**: Ca²⁺ (valency 2) + O²⁻ (valency 2) → CaO
- **Na₂O**: Na⁺ (valency 1) + O²⁻ (valency 2) → Na₂O

### Formulae of Compounds with Polyatomic Ions
- **Ca(OH)₂**: Ca²⁺ + 2OH⁻ → Ca(OH)₂
- **Mg(NO₃)₂**: Mg²⁺ + 2NO₃⁻ → Mg(NO₃)₂
- **Na₂SO₄**: 2Na⁺ + SO₄²⁻ → Na₂SO₄
- **CaCO₃**: Ca²⁺ + CO₃²⁻ → CaCO₃

## 3.6 Molecular Mass and Formula Unit Mass

### Molecular Mass
- Sum of atomic masses of all atoms in one molecule
- Calculated by adding atomic masses of all atoms in formula
- Unit: **unified mass (u)** or **atomic mass unit (amu)**
- Example: H₂O = 2(1) + 16 = **18 u**
- Example: CO₂ = 12 + 2(16) = **44 u**
- Example: H₂SO₄ = 2(1) + 32 + 4(16) = **98 u**

### Formula Unit Mass
- Sum of atomic masses of all atoms in formula unit (for ionic compounds)
- Same calculation as molecular mass but for ionic compounds
- Example: NaCl = 23 + 35.5 = **58.5 u**
- Example: CaCl₂ = 40 + 2(35.5) = **111 u**
- Example: Al₂O₃ = 2(27) + 3(16) = **102 u**
